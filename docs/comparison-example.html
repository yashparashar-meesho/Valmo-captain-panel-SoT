Schematics only. Pick one or two; the survivors get a full-size greyed wireframe on the real skeleton, where layout can actually be judged. The rail shows which module each one lives in.

What the captain does — the steps, in order, from what sets them off to the point where they know what to do. If a shape runs out of steps before that, it is not finished.

What the captain does

  1. They open DC Capacity.
  2. They tap the Pilot Productivity tab.
  3. They see the state — OK, warning, cut or restored.
  4. They read which pilots are over 280.
  5. They know how many have to come down.

What it does

  • Cut sits beside the capacity it changes
  • Tab strip already exists, two modules use it
  • Per-pilot detail is not excluded here — that applied to the other screen

What it costs

  • Adds a second screen to a module captains open rarely
Best if the cut is read as a capacity event.

What the captain does

  1. They open Pilot Management, which they do anyway.
  2. They read the two new columns beside each pilot.
  3. They see who is over 280.
  4. They go somewhere else to find out what the cut is.

What it does

  • Nothing new to learn; captains already live here
  • Daily rhythm matches the roster's

What it costs

  • Roster's decision is “who is on my team”, not “who is overloaded”
  • The cut has nowhere to be explained
Best if the fix is managing people, not capacity.

What the captain does

  1. They open Growth Dashboard.
  2. They see orders are down.
  3. A section says a capacity cut caused it.
  4. They read how many weeks it has run.

What it does

  • Growth Dashboard already explains why orders moved
  • Weekly scope matches the cut's

What it costs

  • Would be a third section — no screen has three
  • Diagnostic module; captains cannot act from here
Best if the cut is only ever explained, never managed.

What the captain does

  1. They open DC Capacity to set a number.
  2. An alert says a cut is active.
  3. They follow the link to find the detail.
  4. If they do not follow it, they stop here.

What it does

  • Warns at the exact moment of committing
  • No new screen anywhere

What it costs

  • Splits one subject across two modules
  • Neither page owns the story
Best if the warning matters more than the detail.

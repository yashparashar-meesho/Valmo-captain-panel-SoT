Every screen in the catalogue: what it is for first, then what it is made of. Read the UX to decide whether something belongs; read the structure to build it.

The belonging test

Something belongs on a page when it serves that page's decision. If it serves a different decision, it belongs on the page that owns that decision - or on a new one.

Then check the two lists:

  • already in answers -> it exists; extend it rather than adding beside it
  • listed under not here -> it was considered and rejected; read why before re-proposing it
  • in neither -> genuinely new, and the decision above is the test

answers and not here are scoped to one screen, never to the module. "DC Capacity does not show per-pilot detail" excludes it from that screen. A second screen in the same module - a tab - is a different screen, and the exclusion does not reach it. Reading a screen's exclusions as the module's is how a tab stops being considered.

Once the page is settled, section-rules.md decides whether it is a new section or baked into an existing one.

How a captain sees the orders they are winning against the ceiling their area can produce, and what is costing them the difference.

Who — The captain who wants more volume and more earnings, and needs to know what is standing in the way.

DC Capacity

dc-capacity - 1 screen

How a captain sets the delivery and pickup volume their DC will take, against the demand in their area and the pilots they have.

Who — The captain who runs the DC — the person accountable for whether the centre can absorb the volume it is offered.

How often — Rarely, and never casually. Capacity is a commitment: it takes effect the next day and the matching volume only arrives 5–7 days later. Visited when demand shifts or the pilot roster changes, not as part of a daily routine.

Journey — Arrived at from Growth Dashboard, after seeing orders were missed and wanting to know whether the ceiling was the cause. Leaves having committed a number, with nothing to check until the volume moves days later.

DC Capacity

dc-capacity

Job — "Tell me how much I should take on, and let me commit to it."

Decision it supports — What delivery and pickup numbers should this DC commit to for the coming week?

Answers today

  • How much demand is there in my area?
  • How many pilots do I actually have?
  • What does the panel think I should take?
  • If I pick a different number, how far off am I — and in what currency (orders short, or pilots short)?

Deliberately not here

  • History of past capacity commitments — the page is about the next decision, not a record of previous ones.
  • Per-pilot detail — that is Pilot Management's decision, not this one.
  • Any view of whether the last commitment paid off — that is Growth Dashboard's job, and splitting it across two pages would mean neither owns it.
